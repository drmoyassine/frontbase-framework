Environmental Monitoring

Remote monitoring that survives the wilderness.

Detecting wildfires, tracking flood levels, and monitoring air quality happens deep in forests and remote regions where the nearest cell tower is 50 miles away. Streamline your climate tech with edge nodes that buffer data, process AI anomalies locally, and only use precious satellite bandwidth when disaster strikes.

You Can't Stop What You Can't See

Critical environmental data is useless if it's trapped in a disconnected sensor.

Wildfire Detection Delays

Standard optical sensors stream gigabytes of raw video to the cloud for smoke detection. Over slow satellite links, imagery gets compressed or dropped, delaying response times when every minute counts.

Flood Sensor Washouts

During severe storms, cell networks are the first infrastructure to fail. River gauges reliant on constant 5G connectivity go dark precisely when municipalities need flood data the most.

BatteryWarning

Power & Bandwidth Drain

Constantly pinging a central server drains battery life on solar-powered wilderness hardware. Devices die months before their scheduled maintenance, leaving massive blind spots.

Edge Intelligence in the Wild

Process data locally. Transmit only the emergencies.

On-Device Anomaly ML

Run AI models directly on the sensor edge. Cameras detect smoke patterns locally and only transmit a 5KB coordinate alert, rather than streaming 4K video over satellite.

Crisis Data Buffering

When storms knock out network bridges, SQLite databases on the edge continue to aggregate river levels. When the link returns, everything syncs chronologically to the cloud.

Optimized Transmissions

Edge nodes consolidate sensor readings. Instead of 10,000 pings a day, Frontbase workflows bundle data into a single, efficient burst — extending battery life by years.

When Centralized IT Shuts Down the Factory Floor

Manufacturing is the #1 most targeted industry for ransomware. 47% of all manufacturing breaches in 2024 were ransomware.

Lock

Norsk Hydro — $75M (March 2019)

LockerGoga ransomware forced the aluminum giant to shut down its global IT network and switch 35,000 employees to manual operations. Smelting plants reverted to pen-and-paper control. Total cost: $63–75 million in lost margins, production volume, and remediation.

AlertOctagon

Toyota — $356M/day (2022–2023)

In 2022, a cyberattack on a single supplier (Kojima Industries) forced Toyota to halt all 28 production lines across 14 plants — cutting global output by one-third. In 2023, a system 'glitch' in centralized parts ordering shut down 14 factories again. One day of lost production: ~$356M.

TrendingDown

MKS Instruments — $200M+ (Feb 2023)

Ransomware severely disrupted production systems at MKS Instruments, contributing to a 20% decline in quarterly revenue and losses exceeding $200 million. Earlier that year, Brunswick Corporation lost at least $85 million. Clorox lost $356 million in sales.
